# wet cat food: how to cut the monthly cost without switching brands or overstocking

Wet food is the one recurring pet expense that behaves like a subscription nobody asked for. You buy the same case every few weeks, the price creeps, and the only lever most people pull is switching to a cheaper brand — which usually ends with a cat turning its nose up and a half-eaten case going in the bin.

There's a more boring fix, and it works on whatever brand your cat already eats: get the buying mechanics right. Case size, autoship discounts, and retailer cashback stack, and none of them require changing the food.

This is about how that actually works, where it breaks, and what the numbers look like on a real cat household.

## Start with cans per month, not price per can

Price per can is the wrong unit. Wet food packs anywhere from roughly 25 to 40 calories per ounce depending on whether it's a dense pâté or chunks sitting in gravy, so two cans of different brands can be wildly different amounts of food.

A useful rough planning figure for a healthy adult cat on an all-wet diet is somewhere in the region of 6–8 ounces a day, but the calorie density on the specific can is what decides it, and the feeding chart on the label is the authority. Kidney and senior formulas are often lower-calorie per ounce, which means more cans, not fewer.

What that means for the case you're buying:

- **24 × 3 oz cans** runs about 72 ounces, so a single cat on an all-wet diet gets through a case in roughly 10 to 12 days.
- **24 × 5.5 oz cans** runs about 132 ounces, closer to three weeks for one cat, and around 10 days in a two-cat household where wet food is the whole diet.

ShopBack's own Chewy guide uses a similar framing: a 24-can case lasts a two-cat household on daily wet food about two to three weeks. The point isn't the exact number, it's the multiples. A two-cat household burning through a case every 10 days is buying 36 cases a year. At that volume, every percentage point of discount is real money, and so is every case that gets bought at the wrong size.

## The four levers, in order of how much they matter

**1. Format and case size.** Cost per ounce typically drops as can size goes up, and 12-can or 24-can cases usually beat singles. The trade-off is counter space and the risk of a cat rejecting a 24-can commitment. This is the biggest lever and the only one that touches the food itself.

**2. Autoship-style discounts.** Chewy's Autoship runs a first-delivery discount that ShopBack's Chewy guides describe as a 5–35% band depending on SKU and promo cycle, then 5% ongoing on recurring deliveries. If you're buying the same wet food every month anyway, that's the deal being handed to you for setting a frequency.

**3. Retailer cashback.** This is the layer that most wet-food buyers skip entirely, and it's where ShopBack comes in.

**4. Credit card rewards on top.** Nothing here cancels anything else out. Autoship, cashback and card rewards come from three different budgets.

## Five habits that quietly cost you money

1. **Buying 3 oz cans at a per-ounce premium** because the case is cheaper up front. Compare cost per ounce, and remember the 3 oz case runs out twice as fast.
2. **Skipping the click-through** on an order you were going to place anyway. It's the single most common reason people's balance stays at zero.
3. **Letting autoship over-order.** If a case lasts 10 days, a monthly frequency leaves you with a growing stack and cash tied up. Set the frequency to the actual consumption and use the reminder email to pause or skip.
4. **Committing 24 cans to an untested food.** Buy a small number first; cats reject food with no regard for your spreadsheet.
5. **Ignoring shipping thresholds.** A cashback of $2.80 doesn't survive a $12 shipping charge. Build the cart to the free-shipping line or use autoship, which usually ships free above a minimum.

## Questions that come up constantly

**How long does an opened can keep?** Refrigerated in a covered container, about three days is the usual guidance. If your cat is a small eater, buy the smaller can format — a third of a 5.5 oz can thrown away twice a week erases more than cashback earns.

**Can I stack a retailer's autoship discount with cashback?** Yes on eligible orders. Chewy's Autoship discount and cashback apply to the same order, which is the whole point of setting the subscription up through a click-through rather than directly.

**Does switching wet food brands break anything?** Not the cashback mechanics. It does break the autoship logic — your discount band applies to the SKU you've committed to, and if you're trialling a new formula, order it one-off first and keep your staple food on the subscription.

**When do I actually get the money?** Tracked within a couple of days in most cases, confirmed in around 90 days. Withdrawal options and minimums differ by market — Singapore has historically required S$10 confirmed cashback, with bank transfer processing inside about 10 working days and a daily withdrawal cap.
